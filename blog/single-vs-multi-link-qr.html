Single-Link vs Multi-Link QR Codes: Which One Should You Use?

📅 Updated September 2026 ⏱️ 3 min read

Should your QR code open one page directly, or show a choice of several links? Both are free to create on MultiLinkQR, and both have a clear purpose. This comparison explains the difference, the trade-offs, and which one to pick for business cards, menus, social media, events and packaging.

The short answer

  • Single-link QR code: the scan opens one page directly. Fastest, simplest, and the right choice when there is only one thing people need.
  • Multi-link QR code: the scan opens a small page with several buttons, and people pick where to go. The right choice when different people want different things.

When a single-link QR code is better

  • There is only one thing to do: "Scan for the menu", "Scan to register", "Scan to download the app".
  • Speed matters: at a busy counter or entrance, people should land on the page instantly.
  • The code is printed very small, for example on a label. A simpler pattern scans more reliably.
  • You link to a page you control (your website or a Google Drive file) that already contains everything people need.

When a multi-link QR code is better

  • Different people want different things. One guest wants the menu, another wants to leave a review, a third wants your Instagram.
  • Limited space: one code on a business card replaces three or four separate codes.
  • You have no website. The multi-link page acts like a mini link page, with no hosting or account needed.
  • Events and campaigns: registration, agenda, map and feedback in one scan, with an optional expiry date after the event.

Examples by use case

  • Restaurant table: single-link to the menu. Restaurant window or receipt: multi-link with menu, ordering and reviews. See the menu QR code guide.
  • Business card: multi-link with website, WhatsApp, LinkedIn and location. See QR codes on visiting cards.
  • Creator: multi-link with all your social media profiles.
  • Survey poster: single-link straight to the Google Form.
  • Product box: multi-link with manual, warranty registration and support.

What both types have in common

  • Free to create and download (PNG or SVG), with no account and no watermark.
  • Same design options: color, background, border, rounded style, size and logo.
  • Both are static: the destination is stored in the code, so it can't be changed after printing, but it also never depends on a subscription.
  • Links must start with https://.

Frequently asked questions

Can I switch a printed code from single to multi-link?

No. The type and links are stored in the printed code. Generate a new code instead.

Does a multi-link code scan slower?

Scanning is just as fast. The only extra step is one tap on a button after the page opens.

Which one gets more engagement?

It depends on your goal. If you want one specific action, a single link avoids distraction. If you want people to connect with you in their preferred way, a multi-link code gives them that choice.
