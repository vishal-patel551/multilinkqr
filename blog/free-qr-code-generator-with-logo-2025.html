Logo Design Best Practices for QR Codes

Not all logos work equally well in QR codes. Follow these guidelines:

Size & Proportion Guidelines

  • Maximum Size: Keep logo under 20-25% of total QR code area
  • Minimum Size: At least 10% for visibility
  • Sweet Spot: 15-18% provides best balance of visibility and scanability
  • Buffer Zone: Maintain clear space around logo for error correction

Logo Shapes That Work Best

  • Circular/Round: Best option—fits naturally in QR code center
  • Square: Works well if kept small enough
  • Shield/Badge: Excellent for professional/institutional brands
  • Horizontal/Wide: Avoid—covers too much QR pattern
  • Vertical/Tall: Avoid—similar scanning issues

Color Considerations

  • High Contrast: Logo should stand out against QR background
  • White Background/Border: Add white padding around logo for separation
  • Brand Colors: Use official brand colors for consistency
  • Avoid Transparency Issues: Ensure logo doesn't blend into QR pattern

Detail & Complexity

  • Simple is Better: Bold, simple logos scan more reliably
  • Avoid Fine Details: Thin lines and small text may not reproduce well
  • High Contrast Elements: Ensure all logo parts are clearly visible
  • Test Readability: Logo should be recognizable at small sizes

Common Logo Placement Mistakes

  • Logo Too Large: Exceeding 25% coverage prevents scanning
  • Complex Logos: Intricate designs with fine details fail at small sizes
  • Low Contrast: Logo blends into QR pattern
  • Wrong Shape: Wide horizontal logos cover critical scanning areas
  • No Background: Logo needs white/light background for separation
  • Poor Quality Image: Low-resolution logos look pixelated and unprofessional
  • Not Testing: Failing to verify scanning before mass printing

Technical Specifications for Logo QR Codes

Print Quality Requirements

  • DPI: 300 DPI minimum for professional printing
  • File Format: PNG or SVG (SVG preferred for scalability)
  • Color Mode: CMYK for offset printing, RGB for digital
  • Resolution: 800x800 pixels minimum for medium-sized prints

Size Guidelines for Different Applications

  • Business Cards: 1.25 x 1.25 inches (logo must be clearly visible)
  • Flyers: 2-3 inches square
  • Posters: 4-6 inches square
  • Banners: Scale proportionally—minimum 6 inches for large venues
  • Product Packaging: Adjust based on package size and viewing distance

Error Correction & Logo Size Balance

QR codes use error correction to remain scannable even with partial obstruction. Understanding this helps optimize logo placement:

  • L Level (7% correction): Not recommended for logo QR codes
  • M Level (15% correction): Standard—supports logos up to 10-12%
  • Q Level (25% correction): Good for logos—supports 15-18%
  • H Level (30% correction): Best for logos—supports up to 20-25%

Troubleshooting: When Logo QR Codes Won't Scan

If your logo QR code isn't scanning properly, try these fixes:

Problem: Code Scans Slowly or Inconsistently

Causes & Solutions:

  • Logo too large: Reduce logo to 15-18% of QR code area
  • Low contrast: Increase contrast between logo and QR background
  • Complex logo design: Simplify logo or use a simplified version for QR codes
  • Insufficient error correction: Ensure generator uses high (H) error correction level

Problem: QR Code Not Scanning at All

Causes & Solutions:

  • Logo covers critical areas: Reposition logo to center or reduce size
  • Print quality too low: Use 300+ DPI for printing
  • QR code too small: Increase to minimum 1.5 inches square
  • Damaged or distorted: Ensure flat, undamaged surface for codes
  • Poor lighting: Add lighting or move to well-lit area

Problem: Logo Appears Pixelated or Blurry

Causes & Solutions:

  • Low-resolution logo file: Use minimum 500x500px PNG or vector SVG
  • JPEG compression artifacts: Convert to PNG with no compression
  • Incorrect scaling: Upload logo at proper resolution, don't upscale small images

Frequently Asked Questions

Does adding a logo make QR codes less reliable?

When done correctly, no. QR codes have built-in error correction that allows up to 30% of the code to be covered while remaining scannable. Professional generators like MultiLinkQR automatically optimize logo size and placement to maintain 99%+ scan reliability across all devices.

Can I change the logo after creating the QR code?

With static QR codes (what MultiLinkQR generates), the logo is embedded in the image and cannot be changed after download. If you need to update your logo, simply generate a new QR code with the updated logo. The benefit is that static codes work forever without requiring any service subscriptions.

What's the smallest size I can print a logo QR code?

For logo QR codes, maintain a minimum print size of 1.5 x 1.5 inches (approximately 4 x 4 cm). This ensures both the QR pattern and your logo remain clearly visible and scannable. On business cards, this is a comfortable size that balances visibility with card space constraints.

Are there copyright concerns with using logos in QR codes?

You should only use logos you own or have permission to use. Using your own business logo is perfectly fine. Using another company's logo without permission could violate trademark law. MultiLinkQR is a tool for creating codes with YOUR logo—we never claim ownership of user-uploaded logos.

Do colored QR codes with logos scan as well as black and white?

Yes, as long as you maintain sufficient contrast. The key is ensuring the darker elements (QR pattern) contrast clearly with the lighter background. For example, dark blue on white background scans just as well as black on white. Avoid low-contrast combinations like yellow on white or dark colors on dark backgrounds.
